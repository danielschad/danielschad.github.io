About the workshop

Research on individual differences in Pavlovian conditioned approach has developed rapidly across several partly overlapping areas: animal work on the neural and neurochemical mechanisms underlying sign- and goal-tracking, computational accounts of why identical cue–reward contingencies can produce different conditioned responses across individuals, and a growing body of human research using measures such as eye-tracking, physical approach tasks, and neural response to cues and rewards.

Although these approaches increasingly inform one another, researchers working with the different paradigms and levels of analysis do not often have the opportunity to meet in the same setting. The aim of this workshop is therefore to bring together researchers across these areas and to create space for detailed discussion of how sign-tracking and goal-tracking are currently conceptualised, measured, and modelled.

A central aim of the meeting will be to compare the different approaches currently used across the field. Lever autoshaping, gaze-based measures, neural measures, physical approach tasks, and formal computational models each capture somewhat different aspects of the phenotype and rely on different assumptions about what sign- and goal-tracking represent and predict. By discussing these approaches alongside one another, we hope to develop a clearer shared picture of where the different lines of work converge, where they differ, and which open questions may be particularly important for future research.

The sessions are intended to emphasise discussion and exchange, with time to explain not only findings but also the practical and methodological details of how the work is carried out.
